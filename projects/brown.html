The most beautiful color I cannot see anywhere else

Today, as I have the past couple weeks, I saw something spectacular, and this picture does not do it justice:

These leaves seem to have an amazing color to them that I do not see anywhere else. It's a yellowish greenish reddish that seems almost translucent in a way, and is simultaneously soft and vivid. What I believe is happening is two things, 1) that the leaves are becoming more translucent as they change, and 2) they are speckled with red and green and yellow, and when viewed from a certain distance, the colors blend enough to form a perception of color more than just speckles, but stay separate enough somehow to avoid becoming a brown.

Brown is not brown

I'm kind of struggling to wrap my head around this, but this led me into a rabbit hole where I discoverd that brown isn't really a color in the standard sense of having a mix of wavelengths that are "brown." That same mix of wavelengths look orangey if you can increase the brightness, so the perception of brown is heavily dependent on how bright the color is compared to its surroundings. I had Claude make this demo to illustrate it.

I also wanted to see where a very brown-looking brown maps onto the chromaticity diagram if you were to make it fully bright, because I think the above brown is more orangey to begin with. I had Claude generate the following graphics to show what a deep brown looks like as you increase its brightness, and where it lies on the chromaticity diagram. I notice when I look at this that the color still looks different in the context of the chromaticity diagram than when shown alone. I never really appreciated how much the visual context influences color perception, it's so stark to see here.

I also hesitate to comment on this and defer to those with more meaningful lived experience, but it is interesting to me that all these colors look like a large range of normal human skin tones. And yet, the chromaticity range actually seems to be rather small. Below is an example of human skin tones levelled to the same luminance. The wide and beatiful range of ways people can be, within a very narrow range of chromaticity, is I think good evidence for just how complex and wondrous the visual system is.
